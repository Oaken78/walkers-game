## Goal
The third-person camera every field scene uses. It follows a target with a short position lag but no rotation lag,
orbits freely with the mouse (yaw free, pitch -10 to 60 deg), zooms 5-12 m on the wheel, narrows to FOV 50 while
aiming, pulls in against terrain with a spring arm (never closer than 2 m), and drifts back behind the target after
1.5 s of mouse idle. It also exposes the crosshair hit point that T06 fires at and a shake that T12 triggers on hits.
It is proven on a stand-in walker in the valley, because the real walker (T03) is built in parallel.

## Rules
1. **Orbit.** Mouse motion turns 0.15 deg per pixel. Moving the mouse right turns the view right; moving it up tilts
   the view up (pitch toward -10). Yaw is free; pitch is clamped to -10..60. Rotation has no lag: the new angles show on
   the same frame. Mouse motion counts only while the mouse is captured.
2. **Follow.** The pivot = target interpolated position + `target_offset`, followed with an exponential lag:
   `pivot += (goal - pivot) * (1 - exp(-delta / position_lag))`. At a steady 4.5 m/s the pivot trails by about
   0.45 m. The rig never inherits the target's rotation (`top_level`).
3. **Zoom.** `zoom_in` / `zoom_out` change `distance` by `zoom_step`, clamped to 5-12 m, eased over `zoom_time`.
4. **Spring arm.** The arm collides with layer 1 only, using a sphere of `collision_radius`. Its length never drops
   below `collision_min_distance`, and the camera never ends up behind rock seen from the pivot.
5. **Aim.** While `aim` is held, the FOV eases from 70 to 50 over `aim_fov_time`; on release it eases back. The
   distance does not change. (The -40 % turn rate is T03's job.)
6. **Recentre.** After `recenter_delay` s with no mouse motion, while the target moves faster than
   `recenter_min_target_speed`, the yaw turns toward behind the target's facing at `recenter_rate_deg`. Any mouse
   motion stops it and restarts the timer. Pitch is left alone.
7. **Shake.** `add_shake` offsets the camera (h/v offset, not the pivot) by up to `amplitude`, decaying to 0 over
   `duration`, from a seeded `RandomNumberGenerator`.
8. **Mouse capture.** The mouse is captured on ready when `capture_mouse` is set. `pause` releases it and a left
   click in the window captures it again (T12's pause menu replaces this later).
9. **Pure maths in `OrbitMath`** (`class_name OrbitMath extends RefCounted`, static funcs): mouse to angles with the
   clamp, the follow factor, the camera offset from yaw, pitch and length, the arm clamp, the recentre step, the shake
   envelope, and a pinhole projection (point, camera transform, FOV, viewport height -> pixel position) for the framing
   test. The node script only wires these to the scene, so the unit tests cover the behaviour without scenes.

## Stand-in walker and test scene (`scenes/camera/camera_test.tscn` + `scripts/camera/camera_test.gd`)
- Instances `scenes/world/valley.tscn` and waits for `built`.
- A stand-in walker at the size of the largest M0 build (the Strider): a 2.2 x 0.6 x 1.4 m box whose origin is 0.96 m
  above the ground, six 0.25 x 0.20 x 0.25 m feet at x = +/-2.4 m and z = -2.0, 0, +2.0 m, and a thin line from the
  body to each foot. Colours from GDD 10 (body `#E6E1D6`, feet `#FF8A3D`).
- Helpers the scenario calls: `place_standin(spot)` with `"workshop"` (near `WorkshopSite`) and `"cliff"` (4 m from the
  west wall, facing along it); `walk_standin(speed, seconds)` (a straight walk at constant speed, in physics ticks);
  `hold_action(action, pressed)` (`Input.action_press/release`; the harness cannot hold an action across steps).
- Read-only properties for `assert_prop`: `lag_m` (pivot to target + offset), `camera_clear` (a world raycast from
  the pivot to the camera hits nothing), `yaw_error_deg` (the camera yaw minus behind-the-target yaw, wrapped to
  +/-180).
